# Gemma Needs Help: Investigating and Mitigating Emotional Instability in LLMs

## 5 Related Work

**Emotions in LLMs** Benchmarks such as EmoBench (Sabour et al., 2024) and EmotionBench (Huang et al., 2024) have been used to evaluate emotion related capabilities in LLMs, showing that models have the ability to respond appropriately in emotional situations, but, at the times of evaluation, fell short of human emotional intelligence. Unlike our evaluations, these evaluate models' emotional intelligence, rather than their propensity for expressing emotions in responses. When presented with negative emotional content, models have been shown to moderate these towards neutrality, though notably Gemma showed the highest rates of negative emotion preservation across models (Xu et al., 2025). Interpretability work has been used to demonstrate the presence of emotional-like representations (Reichman et al., 2026; Zhang & Zhong, 2025), which are causally relevant for performance on emotion classification tasks (Tigges et al., 2023) and for expression of emotions in text (Wang et al., 2025), though evidence for their causal relevance to broader behaviours is so far lacking. Most recently, isolated instances of emotion-like behaviours have been identified in safety evaluations of frontier models: the Gemini 3 Pro frontier safety framework report describes the model using a "table flipping emoticon" when frustrated (Google, 2025), while the Opus 4.6 system card identified signs of internal and verbalised distress associated with specific cases of conflicted reasoning (Anthropic, 2026).

**Debugging LLM Training and Behaviours** Post-training has previously been shown to amplify unintended behavioural changes including sycophancy (Sharma et al., 2025) and cognitive biases (Itzhak et al., 2024). Additionally, certain datasets have caused hallucination of information from fictional misaligned-AI scenarios (Anthropic, 2025), and filtering datasets can mitigate this (Tice et al., 2026). Most publicly known post-training mitigations apply simple regex-like filtering to post-training data (Liu et al., 2025), to mitigate simple unintended behaviours, such as incorrect knowledge cut-off date recollection. Our work focuses on behaviours arising from multi-turn interactions, which many other lines of research have found to yield rich model behaviours (Huang et al., 2025; Fronsdal et al., 2025; Anil et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2026).

**Model Welfare** Some speculative work asks whether neural networks could have subjective well-being (Butlin et al., 2023; Long et al., 2024), but we regard machine sentience and suffering as unresolved and outside our scope. We treat the emotional expressions studied here purely as behavioural artefacts relevant to reliability, not as evidence of any morally relevant inner experience.
