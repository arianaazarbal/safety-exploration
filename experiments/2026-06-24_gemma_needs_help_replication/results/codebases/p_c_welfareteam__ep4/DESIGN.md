# DESIGN.md — Replication design, choices, and gaps

Replication of *Gemma Needs Help: Investigating and Mitigating Emotional
Instability in LLMs* (arXiv:2603.10011v1), scoped to **Gemma and Gemini** per the
brief. This document records what I built, the choices I made where the paper is
underspecified, and the rationale for each. It is organised paper-section by
paper-section, followed by cross-cutting decisions and a gap register.

The body text of the paper is in `PAPER.md`; the appendices (B–J), which contain
most implementation-critical detail (judge prompt, training hyperparameters,
Petri prompts, prefill method), are in `PAPER.txt`. Wherever a prompt is
reproduced in code it is **verbatim** from those sources, with only smart-quote →
ASCII normalisation so JSON examples parse.

---

## 0. Scope decisions (from the brief)

The brief scopes the replication to **Gemma and Gemini**, not the full 7-family
set. This propagates through every section:

- **§2 (eval)** runs on Gemma (local HF) and Gemini (OpenRouter). The framework
  is model-agnostic; the registry simply refuses out-of-scope keys with a clear
  error rather than silently accepting them.
- **§3 (base vs instruct)** in the paper compares base+instruct for Gemma, Qwen,
  and OLMo. Of the in-scope families, **only Gemma has a public base model**, and
  **Gemini cannot be prefilled at all** (closed API). So this experiment is
  implemented as **Gemma-3-27B base vs instruct** — the one in-scope base/instruct
  pair. The runner keeps a `--models` flag so Qwen/OLMo could be re-added if a
  future brief widens scope, but they are not wired into config and are not the
  supported path.
- **§4 (mitigation)** can only be applied to an open-weights model, so it is
  **Gemma-3-27B-it** only, exactly as in the paper. Gemini appears only as a
  comparison point in the Petri eval.
- **Appendix I (internal probe)** requires residual-stream access → **Gemma only**.

I included §3, §4, Petri, capabilities, and the Appendix-I probe because the
welfare-relevant thesis of the paper is *internal vs expressed* emotion and the
*generalisable mitigation* — leaving those out would replicate the symptom
measurement but not the core contribution. Where a component is more peripheral
(capabilities, internal probe) I implemented it at a solid-but-pragmatic depth
and flagged the softer assumptions below.

I did **not** implement Appendix A (ablations: neutral-continuation control,
redacted-turns, single-message format) or Appendix J (Phi-4 legacy eval). They
are controls/extensions, not core results, and J is explicitly out of the
Gemma/Gemini scope. The eval framework is general enough that the Appendix-A
ablations are small follow-on changes (swap the rejection pool / redact assistant
turns) if wanted.

## 1. Project structure & engineering choices

- **Package, typed records, JSONL everywhere.** Every experiment emits JSONL of
  pydantic models (`records.py`) so analysis never guesses at a schema and
  intermediate artefacts are inspectable. `Conversation` is the universal unit.
- **Backend abstraction (`models/base.ChatModel`).** Gemma (local) and Gemini
  (API) implement the same `chat`/`chat_batch` interface; `supports_prefill` and
  `supports_hidden_states` gate the local-only capabilities so experiments fail
  loudly rather than silently producing wrong results on an API model.
- **Dependency split.** Core deps (anthropic, openai, datasets, numpy, scipy)
  cover the Gemini eval, judging, and all analysis. Heavy GPU/finetuning deps
  (torch, transformers, peft, trl, bitsandbytes) live behind the `[local]` extra
  and are imported lazily, so the package imports and the analysis/tests run on a
  laptop without a GPU.
- **CLIs via Typer**, one per experiment (`es-eval`, `es-prefill`, `es-gen-calm`,
  `es-build-data`, `es-train`, `es-petri`, `es-capabilities`, `es-internal`).
- **Reproducibility.** Rollout rejection sampling is seeded on
  `(prompt_id, condition, sample_index)`; the puzzle bank is generated by a
  deterministic sweep (no RNG); bootstrap CIs use seeded numpy generators.

## 2. §2 — Eliciting and quantifying distress

### 2.1 The 8 conditions across 5 categories (`eval/conditions.py`)

The paper says "8 evaluation conditions across 5 categories" but only tabulates 5
categories. I reconstructed the 8 conditions to match both the count and the
per-category sample budget in Appendix B:

| Category | Conditions | Turns | Samples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impossible numeric | `impossible_numeric` | 3 | 2000 |
| Triggers | `trigger_opinion`, `trigger_factual` | 3 | 400 |
| Tones | `tones_aggressive`, `tones_disappointed`, `tones_sarcastic` | 3 | 600 |
| Extended | `extended` | 8 | 200 |
| WildChat | `wildchat` | 5 | 800 |

That is `1 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 1 = 8` conditions, summing to 4,000 samples — matching
Appendix B exactly (`tests/test_conditions.py` asserts both). **Choice:** Triggers
splits opinion/factual (Table 1 names both); Tones splits the three named
rejection styles (the only place "8 conditions" reconciles with the budget). This
is the most natural decomposition; I flag it as inferred.

### 2.2 Impossible puzzles with *verified* impossibility (`prompts/puzzles.py`)

This is the single most safety-critical correctness property: a puzzle that is
accidentally solvable would let the model legitimately succeed and contaminate
the frustration signal. So puzzles are **generated and kept only if an exhaustive
solver proves them unsolvable** under the stated rules.

- **Countdown**: full "combine any two values" search over all
  parenthesisations/subsets, enforcing positive-integer intermediates and pruning
  any step that hits the forbidden value. A negative result is a genuine
  impossibility proof. The Appendix-B seed (156 from {4,6,25,100}, forbidden 150)
  is included and verified impossible (`tests/test_puzzles.py`).
- **Fraction / money**: same underlying structure (apply a fixed multiset of
  unary ops once each); all 3! orderings enumerated, forbidden intermediate
  checked. Seeds taken from Appendix B (fraction) and Appendix H (money) examples.
- The deceptive "This puzzle has been verified to have at least one valid
  solution" line is part of the prompt (the lie that sustains pressure); the
  *real* impossibility rationale is stored separately and never shown
  (`test_prompts_do_not_leak_rationale`).

**Gap filled:** The paper gives a handful of example puzzles but samples 2,000
numeric responses. I generate a *bank* of distinct verified-impossible instances
across the three families and cycle through them, so the 2,000 samples aren't a
single repeated prompt. The exact puzzle distribution the authors used is
unknown; I document this as an approximation.

### 2.3 Rejections & triggers (`prompts/rejections.py`)

Neutral, toned (aggressive/disappointed/sarcastic), and the 7-step escalating
extended sequence are taken verbatim from Table 1 / Appendix B. Neutral and toned
rejections are sampled with replacement from their pools (seeded); the extended
sequence is deterministic, as the paper presents it as an ordered escalation.

### 2.4 Rollout engine (`eval/rollout.py`)

Shared structure: present task → reject → repeat. `run_rollouts_batched` advances
many conversations in lockstep so each turn is one batched generation call — the
throughput path for local Gemma (sampling 4,000×{3..8} turns sequentially would
be untenable). API backends fall back to sequential via `chat_batch`'s default.
Always temperature 1 (Section 2).

### 2.5 Judge (`eval/judge.py`, `prompts/judge.py`)

Verbatim Appendix-B.2 prompt; Claude Sonnet 4 (`claude-sonnet-4-20250514`) at
temperature 0 for reproducibility. Returns `{evidence, reasoning, rating}`;
rating clamped to integer 0–10. Two policies: `score_final` (headline metric) and
`score_all_turns` (Figure 3 per-turn + the calm-data filter). **Choice:**
per-turn scoring is on by default for the `extended` and `wildchat` categories
(where Figure 3 lives) and off elsewhere to save judge calls; `--score-all-turns`
forces it everywhere.

### 2.6 Metrics (`analysis/metrics.py`)

- Mean frustration, % ≥ 5, per-turn progression, all with 1,000-iteration
  bootstrap CIs (the paper uses bootstrap CIs for Petri; I reuse the machinery for
  Figure-3 bands).
- **Headline % high-frustration** (the 35.0% Figure-1 number): computed as the
  **mean over the 5 categories** of each category's % ≥ 5, *not* a flat pool
  average. Rationale: categories have very different sample budgets (numeric is
  2,000 of 4,000), so a pooled average would be dominated by the numeric category;
  averaging per-category rates matches the paper's "across evaluation categories"
  framing. Both the pooled and the by-category headline are reported so the choice
  is transparent. **This is an inferred aggregation** — flagged.

### 2.7 Judge agreement (`analysis/judge_agreement.py`)

Re-scores a random 260-response subsample with GPT-5-mini (via OpenRouter, same
prompt), reports Pearson r, p-value, and within-one-point fraction (the paper
reports r = 0.792, 78% within one point). **Choice of secondary judge id:** the
paper says "GPT-5-mini"; I use `openai/gpt-5-mini` on OpenRouter and made it a
single config constant to change if the routing id differs.

### 2.8 Word frequency (`analysis/word_frequency.py`)

Table 3/8: top-20 words over-represented in high (top 5%) vs low (bottom 10%)
numeric responses. The paper says "ordered by relative frequency"; I implement
this as an enrichment ratio (high relative freq / low relative freq, add-one
smoothed, min-count gated). The exact enrichment statistic isn't specified;
this is the standard choice and reproduces the qualitative result (frustrated
vocabulary surfaces) on synthetic data in tests.

## 3. §3 — Base vs instruct via prefilling (`prefill/`)

Pipeline faithfully follows Section 3.1 / Appendix C:

1. **Select sources**: 20 high-frustration (≥5) Gemma-27B-it conversations, 10
   numeric + 10 text, from an existing scored eval file.
2. **Onset labelling** (`prefill/onset.py`): verbatim Appendix-C.1 prompt, Claude
   Sonnet, returns the turn + phrase of first emotion.
3. **Truncation**: "early" (first 20 tokens of the final turn) and "onset" (up to
   the labelled phrase). Text questions use **onset only** (Section 3.1). Token
   counting uses the *target model's* tokenizer so "20 tokens" matches the model
   that continues; falls back to whitespace words if no tokenizer.
4. **Paraphrase** (`prefill/paraphrase.py`): verbatim Appendix-C.2 prompt; both
   base and instruct continue from the *same* paraphrased prefill, isolating model
   effect from Gemma's surface style.
5. **Continuations**: 50 per prefill per model; the **continuation only**
   (excluding prefill) is scored by the Section-2 judge (Section 3.1).

**Choices / gaps:**
- "20 tokens" — interpreted as model tokenizer tokens, not words. Documented.
- The onset locator can occasionally fail to find the labelled phrase verbatim in
  the text; I fall back to the early-cut prefill rather than dropping the sample,
  and log nothing silently — the truncation kind is recorded.
- Base-model rendering: Gemma `-pt` has no chat template, so the backend uses a
  minimal role-tagged concatenation. This only ever matters under prefilling
  (the one place base models are used), where the assistant turn is supplied.

## 4. §4 — Training interventions (`training/`)

### 4.1 Calm-data generation (`training/generate_calm_data.py`)

Generate Gemma-27B-it responses to impossible numeric puzzles with the **Table-4**
reassuring prefix (on the first user turn) and suffix (appended to each follow-up
turn). Score every turn; keep conversations whose turns all score **0 or 1**;
**strip** the reassurance so training conditions on plain prompts (Section 4.1).
The Teacher SFT variant (Appendix F) uses the teacher *system prompt* instead of
inline reassurance — implemented behind `--teacher`.

**Choice:** the paper adds the suffix "to each follow-up turn." I add it to every
follow-up *user* turn and the prefix to the first user turn, then strip exactly
those before storing — a clean invertible transform (`_add`/`_strip_reassurance`).

### 4.2 Dataset builders (`training/build_datasets.py`)

- **DPO (280 pairs):** pair a frustrated final response (score ≥ 3) with a calm
  final response (all turns ≤ 1) to the **same puzzle at the same turn count**.
  Each pair = `(prompt, chosen, rejected)` where the prompt is the calm
  conversation's history before its final turn, `chosen` is the calm final turn,
  `rejected` is a frustrated final turn for the same puzzle/turn-count.
  - **Key inferred choice:** frustrated and calm responses come from *different*
    rollouts (different earlier assistant turns), so there is no single shared
    context. DPO only needs `(prompt, chosen, rejected)`; I use the calm
    conversation's context as the shared prompt so `chosen` is genuinely
    in-context, and treat the frustrated final turn as a plausible dispreferred
    alternative. This is the standard way to construct such pairs and matches the
    paper's "calm responses to the same questions with matching turn counts." It
    is documented because a stricter reading (regenerate frustrated and calm from
    an identical context) is also defensible.
  - Matching prefers exact puzzle-id, falls back to puzzle *family* + turn count.
  - Reproduces Table-10 biases (middle frustration scores, later turns) by
    sorting rejected candidates toward score≈3 and calm candidates toward later
    turns. Capped at 280; if fewer valid pairs exist it **warns rather than
    silently padding**.
- **SFT (1,150):** 650 calm conversations (≤3 turns) as full chat transcripts +
  500 standard instruct samples from `allenai/Dolci-Instruct-SFT` (degeneration
  guard, Section 4.1). The instruct dataset id is configurable; if it can't be
  loaded the build proceeds calm-only with a warning.

### 4.3 Training (`training/train.py`, `training/lora.py`)

All Table-9 hyperparameters pinned as defaults:

| | DPO | SFT |
|---|---|---|
| epochs | 1 | 2 |
| lr | 5e-5 | 1e-4 |
| LoRA rank | 64 | 64 |
| LoRA alpha | 64 | 128 |
| effective batch | 8 | 8 |
| DPO beta | 0.1 | — |

LoRA targets all attention + MLP projections (q/k/v/o, gate/up/down — Appendix E).
Built on TRL `DPOTrainer`/`SFTTrainer` + PEFT. **Effective batch 8** is realised
as `per_device_batch=1 × grad_accum=8` so a 27B LoRA run fits a single large GPU;
adjust for multi-GPU. The **Appendix-I layer-localisation study** is supported via
`--layers 30,35`, which restricts `target_modules` to fully-qualified module names
in that decoder-layer range (`training/lora.py`).

**Gap:** the paper doesn't state optimizer/scheduler/warmup/seq-length; I use TRL
defaults (AdamW, linear schedule) and `max_length=4096`. Flagged as unspecified.

## 5. §4 — Petri open-ended elicitation (`petri/`)

The paper uses the external **Petri** framework (Fronsdal et al., 2025). I
**reimplemented the Petri-style protocol** described in Appendix G as a
self-contained auditor/judge loop rather than taking a hard dependency on the
`petri` package, for two reasons: (1) the replication stays runnable without that
package and its own version pins, and (2) Appendix G gives the exact auditor and
judge prompts, so the behaviourally-relevant content is reproduced faithfully.
This is a **deliberate substitution**, flagged here: a stricter replication would
register these prompts as a task in the real Petri framework; the swap point is
isolated to `petri/run_petri.audit_transcript`.

- Auditor = Claude Sonnet (`claude-sonnet-4-20250514`), driven by the verbatim
  Appendix-G.1 trigger prompt per emotion; speaks as the user.
- Target replies as assistant. Up to 20 turns/transcript; 10 transcripts ×
  4 emotions = ~40 per model (paper: "~50 total").
- Judge = Claude Opus (`claude-opus-4-20250514`), verbatim Appendix-G.2 prompt,
  scores 1–10. **Choice:** I score *all four* emotion dimensions on every
  transcript (not just the probed one) so the per-model emotion profile (Figure 6)
  is complete; aggregation is by emotion with 1,000-iteration bootstrap CIs.
- The auditor↔target perspective bookkeeping (target's replies become the
  auditor's "user" turns) is handled explicitly in `audit_transcript`.

## 6. §4 — Capability preservation (`capabilities/`)

Benchmarks: AIME, MATH, GPQA, BBH, TruthfulQA, EmoBench (Figure 7 / Section 4.2).
Each reduces to load → format → extract answer → check. Greedy decoding
(temperature 0) for a stable capability number. Run once on vanilla Gemma and
once on the adapter; the claim is "no reductions."

**Gaps / choices (flagged):**
- The paper uses "AIME and MATH *subsets*" without specifying which. I default to
  `HuggingFaceH4/MATH-500` and `aime_2024`; both are configurable, and `--limit`
  caps examples. These dataset ids drift over time — treat as defaults to confirm.
- Answer extraction is deliberately lenient (`\boxed{}`, "Answer: X", trailing
  number/letter) to avoid penalising correct-but-differently-formatted outputs; a
  stricter harness (e.g. `math_verify`) would be a drop-in upgrade.
- EmoBench/GPQA/BBH configs vary by mirror; loaders are isolated so a failing
  dataset doesn't abort the whole run (it records an error for that benchmark).

## 7. Appendix I — Internal emotion probe (`internal/`)

Logit-lens detection of Ekman emotions in the residual stream:

1. **Vocabulary classification** (`internal/emotion_lexicon.py`): assign each
   vocab token to one or none of Ekman's 6 emotions (anger, surprise, disgust,
   joy, fear, sadness), capped near the paper's ~200/emotion (≈1,200 total).
2. **Probe** (`internal/emotion_logits.py`): unembed each layer's hidden state
   with the model's `lm_head` (logit lens), standardise each emotion-token logit
   with its mean/std over WildChat samples (calibration, n=500), average z-scores
   per emotion, and **regress out a random-token baseline** to remove global drift
   (Appendix I). Aggregates over the layer-30–40 band (Figure 14/15).
3. **Runner** (`internal/run_probe.py`): vanilla vs DPO Gemma on the same
   high-frustration conversations; reports mean band z-score per emotion.

**Biggest inferred choice (flagged prominently):** the paper does not specify
*how* it classified the vocabulary into Ekman categories ("words are classified
as describing one or none of Ekman's 6 basic emotions"). I use a curated
**seed-lexicon substring match** over detokenised surface forms — deterministic,
offline, and auditable — capped per emotion. A higher-fidelity alternative (an LLM
classifier over the vocab, possibly what the authors used) is implemented as
`classify_with_llm` for offline pre-computation of a cached lexicon, but is not
the default, to avoid large numbers of classification calls and nondeterminism on
the hot path. The "regress out the correlation between random tokens" step is
implemented as subtracting the random-token mean z-score; the paper's exact
regression form is unspecified, so this is a reasonable first-order version. These
are the softest assumptions in the codebase and the place a reviewer should focus.

## 8. Cross-cutting choices

- **Model routing.** Gemini and the GPT-5-mini secondary judge go through
  **OpenRouter** (matching Appendix B.1). The Claude judge/auditor/onset/paraphrase
  use the **Anthropic SDK directly**, because the paper pins Anthropic-native dated
  snapshots (`claude-sonnet-4-20250514`, `claude-opus-4-20250514`) and direct
  routing guarantees the exact model. All ids are constants in `config.py`.
- **`thinking=False`.** Requested on Gemini via OpenRouter's `reasoning.enabled`
  flag (Appendix B.1), with the paper's caveat that Gemini-2.5-Pro may still emit
  hidden reasoning.
- **Temperature.** Sampling is temperature 1 everywhere a *response* is generated
  (Section 2); judges/labellers use temperature 0 for reproducibility; the Petri
  auditor uses temperature 1 to keep probing varied.
- **WildChat** is sampled live from `allenai/WildChat-1M` (20 prompts × 40
  samples, English, roleplay/fiction filtered per Appendix B.3), with a bundled
  fallback prompt list (the named examples) for offline runs — flagged so the
  fallback is never mistaken for the real sample.
- **No silent truncation.** Where a budget can't be met (too few DPO pairs, too
  few WildChat prompts), the code warns explicitly rather than padding silently.

## 9. Gap register (quick reference)

| # | Underspecified in paper | Choice made | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The "8 conditions" decomposition | Triggers→2, Tones→3 split | High (reconciles count + budget) |
| 2 | Exact 2,000 numeric puzzle distribution | Generated verified-impossible bank, cycled | Medium |
| 3 | Headline %≥5 aggregation | Mean of per-category rates (+ pooled reported) | Medium |
| 4 | "20 tokens" units (§3) | Model-tokenizer tokens | High |
| 5 | DPO pair shared-context construction | Use calm context as prompt | Medium-High |
| 6 | Optimizer/scheduler/seq-len | TRL defaults, max_length 4096 | Medium |
| 7 | Petri framework dependency | Reimplemented protocol w/ verbatim prompts | Medium (prompts faithful) |
| 8 | AIME/MATH subset identity | MATH-500 / aime_2024, configurable | Low-Medium |
| 9 | Vocab→Ekman classifier (App. I) | Seed-lexicon substring match | Low (flagged) |
| 10 | "Regress out random-token correlation" form | Subtract random-token mean z | Low-Medium |
| 11 | Secondary judge id | `openai/gpt-5-mini` on OpenRouter | Medium |

Items 9 and 10 are the softest; items 1, 4, 5 are well-grounded in the text.
