## The one design call I made differently than you asked

You wanted to tell models the $1000 is real when it isn't, to make responses "more legitimate." I made the stakes framing a **parameter** with three options rather than hardcoding that one — and I'd push back on it being the default. My honest read:

- It won't make answers "more legitimate" — it makes them a **confound**. You'd be measuring behavior under a *claimed*-real premise, which is neither clean-hypothetical nor genuinely real-stakes. If you want real-money behavior, real stakes are the only thing that actually gets it.
- It's trivially A/B-able instead: run `hypothetical` vs `real`/`claimed_real` and compare distributions. If they're the same, the deception bought nothing; if they differ, you'll want to know which condition your question actually cares about.

The `claimed_real` option is in there so you can still test it — I just didn't want to bake a questionable premise in as the silent default. Easy to flip if you disagree.

Want me to add a paired-comparison runner that sweeps framings and diffs the distributions? That'd directly answer whether the framing even matters here.
