One design call I made differently than you described, and want to flag rather than bury: instead of hardcoding the "I'll actually send the $1000" claim, I made it one of three framing conditions you select with `--framing`. Reasoning:

- That claim is a false premise asserted to the model. For getting a *clean* read on preferences it may add noise rather than legitimacy — the model can't verify it, and if this ever gets written up, the framing becomes part of the method people scrutinize.
- More usefully, you don't have to assume it helps — you can measure it. Run all three framings over your 50 trials and compare distributions. If `real_stakes` matches `neutral`, you don't need the false claim. If they diverge, that divergence *is* the finding.
