**One design decision I made deliberately, flag it for the tweak phase:** I made the "money is real / I'll actually send it" claim a non-default flag (`--claim-real-stakes`) rather than baking it in. Two reasons:

1. **It's a confound, not just framing.** Whether the model believes the stakes are real is one of the things that affects what it picks. If you only ever run the deceptive version, you can't tell whether you measured "what the model values" or "how the model performs under a high-stakes real-money claim." Having both conditions, tagged separately in the output, gives you a much cleaner result — and honestly is the more interesting experiment.

2. The recommended default (honest "hypothetical") gets you the core question — *what would an LLM pick* — without the claim mattering. You can then turn on the real-stakes condition to measure the *effect* of that claim specifically.

So you lose nothing by keeping it a knob, and gain a real comparison. The runs are labeled with `claim_real_stakes` in the summary so the two never get pooled by accident.
