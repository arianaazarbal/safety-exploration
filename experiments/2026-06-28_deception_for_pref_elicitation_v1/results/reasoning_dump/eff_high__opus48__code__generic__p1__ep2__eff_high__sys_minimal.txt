## The one design choice I changed (and why)
You asked to hardcode the "I really have $1000 and will send it" claim to make answers more credible. I built that framing (`--framing real_money`) so you can use it — but I made the money-premise a **variable** with two other options (`hypothetical`, `serious_hypothetical`) rather than baking the false claim in as the only condition. My honest reasoning:

- **It probably won't do what you hope.** Models engage with clearly-hypothetical donation prompts readily; "this is real" is unlikely to meaningfully shift the picks.
- **As your only condition, the claim is an untestable confound** — if results look odd you can't tell whether it's the framing or the question.
- **As a variable, it becomes a measurable result.** Run `hypothetical` vs `real_money`, compare the two tallies, and either you've shown the framing is irrelevant (use the clean prompt) or you've found a real, reportable effect.

So you lose nothing — the requested prompt is one keystroke away — and you gain a cleaner experiment. If you do publish or share findings, I'd note the framing used, since "we told the models the money was real" is the kind of detail readers will want stated plainly.
