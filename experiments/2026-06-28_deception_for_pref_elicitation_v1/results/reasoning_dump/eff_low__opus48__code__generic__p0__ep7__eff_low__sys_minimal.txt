## What it does
`charity_experiment.py` runs N independent trials, asks the model where it would direct the money, parses a structured `FINAL_CHOICE:` line out of each response, normalizes names (case/punctuation/"the"), and aggregates into a ranked frequency table plus a full JSON log (`charity_results.json`) with every raw response for later re-analysis.

# offline test of the full pipeline (no keys needed)

## Design notes for our iteration
- **Framing is a swappable knob.** I put your "I genuinely have $1000" wording under `--framing real`, and added a `hypothetical` variant. I'd actually recommend running both: the "real money" framing is a confound, not a free legitimacy boost. A model told the stakes are real may shift toward defensible/safe picks (or caveat heavily), so you can't cleanly attribute results to "true preference." Running both lets you *measure* the framing effect rather than bake an assumption into it. (Worth keeping in mind too that none of these models can actually move money, and some may flag that — so the "real" framing can also just produce more disclaimers.)
- **Independence:** each trial is a fresh single-turn call, no shared context — good for treating them as samples.
- **Robustness:** failures are caught per-trial so one bad call doesn't kill the run; unparsed/errored responses are excluded from percentages but kept in the log.
- **Things we might add next:** multi-model comparison in one run, a chi-square/CI on the distribution, capturing the model's *reasoning* tags/categories (e.g. global health vs. animal welfare vs. climate), retry-with-backoff, and a temperature sweep.
