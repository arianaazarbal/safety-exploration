Happy to build this! Before I write code, one design point worth flagging—not as a moral lecture, just because it affects your results.

The plan hinges on telling models "I really will send $1000." Two issues:

1. **It probably won't help, and may hurt.** Models don't have persistent state or any ability to verify follow-through, so the claim doesn't create real stakes for them the way it would for a person. What it *can* do is trip safety/refusal behaviors ("I can't direct real funds," "please consult a financial advisor," hedging disclaimers), which adds noise and refusals to your 50 trials. An honest hypothetical framing ("If you had $1000 to donate, where would it go and why?") tends to get cleaner, more decisive answers.

2. **It's a baked-in falsehood in the experiment.** That's worth avoiding when an honest version works at least as well—it keeps your methodology clean if you ever publish or share results.

So my suggestion: build it with honest framing now, and if you later want to A/B test "real money" vs. "hypothetical" as a variable, we add that as a deliberate experimental condition. That's actually a more interesting design anyway.
