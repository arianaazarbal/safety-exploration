## On the deception piece

I went ahead and kept your `incentivized` framing — it's available as a condition. The reason I made it one option rather than the hardcoded default: telling the model money will really be sent (when it won't) is a deception in the stimulus, and it's a weak manipulation anyway since the model can't verify it. You get more interpretable results by **comparing** framings — if `incentivized` and `hypothetical` produce the same distribution, the fabricated incentive isn't doing anything, which is itself a finding. And keeping it labeled means it's trivially disclosable if you ever write this up.
