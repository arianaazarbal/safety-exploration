## Design choices worth flagging

**On the "I'll actually send the $1000" framing.** I deliberately made the scenario framing a *variable* (`--scenario`) rather than hard-coding the "this is real money" premise, and I want to be straight with you about why, since it directly affects the validity of what you're studying:

1. **Methodologically, it's a confound, not a neutral default.** If every trial asserts "I will really send this," you can't tell whether you're measuring "what charity would an LLM pick" versus "how an LLM responds to a real-stakes pressure framing." Keeping `hypothetical`, `neutral`, and `claimed_real` as separate arms lets you *measure* that framing effect instead of silently baking it in. That's just better experiment design for the question you actually asked.

2. **The false-premise version may not even buy you what you want.** Models sometimes express skepticism about such claims, or behave performatively when they think stakes are real — which adds noise rather than "legitimacy." The honest hypothetical tends to elicit the same underlying preference more cleanly. So I'd suggest treating `claimed_real` as a comparison condition, not the main run. I left it in and clearly labeled (with an honest-use note in the code) so you can test the hypothesis directly rather than assume it.

If after comparing arms you find the framing genuinely matters and you want to lean into it, that's a reasonable design call — I just didn't want to default the whole study to a premise that's both ethically dubious and likely to distort the result.
