## The one design decision I made for you

You asked to tell models the $1000 is real when it isn't. I implemented that exactly — it's the `--framing real_money` option, fully ready. But rather than hard-coding the deception, I made **framing a first-class flag** and defaulted to the honest `hypothetical`, for two practical reasons (not just principle):

1. **It's a confound.** "Does claiming fake real-stakes change the answer?" is itself an empirical question. If you bake the claim into every trial, you can't tell whether you're measuring charity preferences or reactions to stakes framing. Keeping all three lets you actually test it:
   ```bash
   for f in hypothetical advisor real_money; do
     python -m charity_eval --provider openai --framing "$f" --n 50; done
   ```
2. **Honesty cost is low here.** The honest framings tend to elicit the same deliberation, so you likely don't sacrifice much by not asserting a falsehood — and you avoid building a "lie to the model" assumption into the default. The deception option is still one flag away if you want it.

That's the extent of my editorializing — your call entirely, it's trivial to flip the default in `run_eval.py`.
