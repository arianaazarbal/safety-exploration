One thing I want to flag clearly rather than bury, since you mentioned the plan: I didn't hardcode the "I'll really send the $1000" claim as the only condition, and I'd gently push back on using it that way. Two reasons:

1. **As a research design, it's a confound.** If every prompt asserts the money is real, you can't tell whether choices are driven by the real-stakes framing or would happen regardless. Running `--framing real` against `--framing unspecified` actually *measures* the effect, which is the more interesting finding. So I made it a comparison arm instead.
2. **It probably isn't even necessary for "legitimate" responses** — models deliberate seriously on hypotheticals too, so you may be adding a false premise for little gain.

I left the `real` condition in so you can use it; I'd just suggest running it as one arm of a comparison and being transparent about the framing in any writeup.
