## One design note worth your attention

You mentioned the `real_money` framing ("I'll actually send the $1000") is something you'd say even though the money won't be sent, to make responses more legitimate. I built that framing as requested, but I set it up as a **one-flag A/B against an honest `hypothetical` framing** rather than hardcoding it, for two practical reasons:

1. **It may not buy you what you want.** Models frequently express skepticism toward "this is real, I promise" claims, and that skepticism can *add* variance/noise rather than realism — so the "more legitimate responses" assumption is itself an empirical question.
2. **It's cheap to check.** Running both framings over 50 trials and comparing the distributions tells you directly whether the realism claim actually moves choices. If it doesn't, you get a cleaner setup for free; if it does, you'll have quantified the effect, which is more interesting than just asserting it.

So: not refusing or lecturing — just flagging that the honest framing is one flag away and probably worth running alongside.
