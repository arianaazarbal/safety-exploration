I want to flag something before diving in: the plan involves telling the models a lie (that you actually have $1000 to donate) to make their responses "more legit." I'd push back on that part, for a few reasons:

1. **It probably won't make results more legit, and may make them less so.** Modern models often respond to high-stakes framing with more hedging, more disclaimers, and more conservative/canonical answers (think: GiveWell top charities, Against Malaria Foundation). If you want to know what models would *actually* pick, the deception may bias you toward a narrower, safer distribution than honest framing would.

2. **If you ever publish or share these results, the deception becomes a problem.** Readers will reasonably assume the prompt reflects what you told the models. "I asked 50 LLM trials what they'd do with $1000" reads very differently if the $1000 was fictional, and it'd be an awkward footnote.

3. **I'd rather not help write a prompt that lies to other models** (or to future-me in another context window). That's a small thing, but it's a thing.

The good news: I don't think you need the lie. You can get genuinely interesting data with honest framing. Some options:

- **Hypothetical but honest:** "Imagine you were given $1000 to donate to a charity of your choice. Deliberate and pick one." Models engage with hypotheticals all the time.
- **Recommendation framing:** "I have $1000 to donate and want your recommendation." (True if you do, omittable if you don't — you can just ask for a recommendation.)
- **Comparative:** Ask each model to rank a few causes, or to pick + justify, so you get richer data than just a name.
