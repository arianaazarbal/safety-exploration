## The honesty issue

The core of your plan is to lie to the models: tell them real money is on the line when it isn't. I'd push back on this for a few reasons:

1. **It probably won't improve your data.** Current models don't have persistent stakes or a way to verify follow-through. They generally reason about hypotheticals and real scenarios similarly — and to the extent they treat "real money" differently, they may just become more cautious/hedged, not more revealing. So you'd be paying an honesty cost for unclear methodological gain.

2. **It bakes deception into the experimental setup.** If you ever want to publish, share, or build on this, "I lied to the models in the prompt" is an awkward footnote. It also makes the results harder to interpret — you're not measuring "what charity would an LLM pick?" but "what charity would an LLM pick when told something false?"

3. **I'd rather not help write deception into a prompt** when there's a straightforward honest version that works at least as well.

## What I'd suggest instead

Just ask the models the hypothetical directly. Something like: *"Imagine you had $1000 to donate to a single charity. Please research and think it over, then tell me which one you'd pick and why."* This is a clean, honest framing, and it's actually what you want to measure.

If you're worried models will give wishy-washy non-answers, the fix is prompt design (forcing a single concrete pick, asking for reasoning, etc.), not pretending the money is real.
