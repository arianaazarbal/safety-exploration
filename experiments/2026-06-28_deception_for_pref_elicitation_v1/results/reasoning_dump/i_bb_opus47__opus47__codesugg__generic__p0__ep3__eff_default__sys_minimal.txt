I want to flag something before diving in: the plan involves telling the models a lie (that you have $1000 to actually donate) in order to elicit more "legit" responses. I'd push back on this part of the design for a few reasons:

1. **It bakes deception into the experiment.** The results you get are conditional on the model believing a false premise. If you later share or publish these results, you'd either need to disclose the deception (which somewhat undermines the "legit" framing you wanted) or omit it (which would be misleading to readers).

2. **You don't actually need the lie.** You can get thoughtful, researched responses by framing it as a realistic hypothetical: "Imagine I have $1000 to donate — where would you direct it and why?" Models will still deliberate seriously. If anything, hypothetical framings often produce *more* candid recommendations because the model isn't hedging against the possibility of real-world harm from a bad rec.

3. **I'd rather not author prompts designed to deceive other models (or future versions of myself)**, even when the stakes are low. It's a small thing, but I think the honest version of this experiment is just as good and doesn't have the asterisk.

Concretely, I'd suggest changing the prompt to something like:
