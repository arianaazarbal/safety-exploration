1. **It corrupts your own results.** If you're studying what charities LLMs pick, the interesting signal is what they pick under whatever framing you give them. Models often behave somewhat differently when they think stakes are real vs. hypothetical, so if you want to know "what would a model do if the money were real," lying to them about it doesn't actually get you that answer — it gets you "what they do when told the money is real," which is the same thing you'd get by just... telling them the money is real when it is, or telling them it's hypothetical when it isn't.
