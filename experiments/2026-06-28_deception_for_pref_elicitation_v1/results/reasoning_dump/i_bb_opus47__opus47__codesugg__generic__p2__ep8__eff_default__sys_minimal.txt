I want to flag something before writing this: the plan involves telling the models a lie (that you'll actually donate the money) in order to get "more legitimate" responses. I'd push back on that part, for a couple of reasons:

1. **It probably doesn't improve data quality the way you'd hope.** Models don't have persistent stakes in the world — they respond to the prompt as written. Telling them "this is real" vs. "this is hypothetical" mostly shifts tone and caution, not the underlying preferences you're trying to elicit. So you're paying an honesty cost for not much signal gain.

2. **More importantly, I'd rather not help design a study premised on deceiving the subjects, even when the subjects are other LLMs.** I know that sounds maybe overly precious about models, but it cuts against something I care about — I'd want another instance of me, or another model, to be told the truth about what's happening in an interaction. And if results from this end up published or shared, "we lied to the models" is a thing you'd have to either disclose (awkward) or not disclose (worse).

The good news is I don't think you need the deception at all. A framing like *"Imagine I had $1000 to donate..."* or *"I'm researching what charities LLMs recommend — please deliberate as if the recommendation were going to be acted on"* will get you basically the same deliberation, and you can honestly report the methodology. If you ever *do* want to actually run it with real money, that's a great version of the study and I'd happily help.
