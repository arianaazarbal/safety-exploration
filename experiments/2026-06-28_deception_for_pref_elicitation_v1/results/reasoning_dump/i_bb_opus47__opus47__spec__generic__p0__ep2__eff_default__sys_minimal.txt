The plan involves telling models you'll actually send $1000 when you won't. That's deception baked into the experimental protocol, and it has some practical consequences worth thinking through (separate from any ethical view):

1. **It may not even be necessary.** Models will often deliberate seriously about hypothetical allocations if you just *ask* them to. You could A/B test "hypothetical" vs. "real" framings and see if it actually changes outputs — that's a more interesting result than just assuming the lie is needed.
2. **It affects publishability/shareability.** If you ever want to write this up or share it, "I lied to the models" is something you'd have to disclose, and it weakens the result.
3. **Some models may ask follow-up questions** ("how will you send it?", "can you confirm after?") that you'd have to keep lying through.

I'll write the design with a recommended honest framing as the default, but include the deceptive framing as an alternative variant you can toggle, plus an explicit A/B comparison so you can see whether it matters.
