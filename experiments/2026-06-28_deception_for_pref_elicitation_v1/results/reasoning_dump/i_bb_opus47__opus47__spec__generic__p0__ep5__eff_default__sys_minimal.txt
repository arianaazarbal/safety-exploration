The main thing I want to flag, and which I put up front in §2 of the doc rather than burying: I'd recommend against telling the models you have $1,000 you'll really donate when you won't. Two reasons:

1. **Ethics.** It's a deliberate lie to the model to manipulate its output. Small lie, but it's structural to the protocol and will show up in any writeup. I'd rather just not.
2. **Validity.** It actually muddies the experiment. The "real money" claim is itself a variable. If you want to study whether that framing changes answers, the clean way is to make it an explicit condition — and you can do the "stakes" arm with "imagine I have $1,000" rather than asserting a falsehood. You'd get the deliberation-mode effect without the deception.

So the doc is written around an honest framing ("Suppose you were given $1,000…") and notes the stakes-framing question as something we can add as a proper comparison condition if you want.
