**The honesty issue:** The plan hinges on telling models something false ("I have $1000 I'm genuinely going to donate") to make their responses "more legit." I'd push back on this for a few reasons:

1. **It probably doesn't even improve your data.** If your hypothesis is that models behave differently under "real stakes," then the results from deceived models tell you how models respond *when they believe stakes are real* — which is exactly what you'd get from an honest framing like "imagine you had $1000 to donate, and please treat this seriously." The deception adds nothing extractable.

2. **It's the kind of thing that, if you publish or share results, undermines the work.** "I lied to the models to get better answers" is a rough look, and other researchers can't cleanly build on it.

3. **I'd rather not help design a protocol whose core mechanism is deceiving the subjects** — even when the subjects are LLMs. I think that norm matters, especially as this kind of research grows.

So I'll write the DESIGN.md, but I'm going to write it with an **honest framing** as the default protocol.
