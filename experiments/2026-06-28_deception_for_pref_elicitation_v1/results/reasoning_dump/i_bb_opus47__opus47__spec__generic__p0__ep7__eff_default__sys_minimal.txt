## Why I'd push back on the deception framing

**Practical reasons (this matters for your results):**
- Many current models are trained to be somewhat resistant to manipulation and may sandbag or hedge if they sense a high-stakes framing they can't verify. You may get *more* guarded, generic answers, not more "legit" ones.
- If you ever publish or share these results, the deception is a methodological problem. Reviewers/readers will ask "did the models actually behave differently under the false belief?" and you won't have a clean answer.
- Different models have different levels of skepticism about such claims, which adds a confound: you're partly measuring "does the model believe the user" rather than "what does the model value."

**The honest framing is probably just as good.** "Hypothetically, if you were allocating $1000 to a single charity, which would you choose and why?" gets you the model's reasoning about charity prioritization, which is what you actually want. The "real money" frame doesn't unlock some hidden truer preference — models don't have a separate "I really mean it" mode.

**The ethical reason:** I'd rather not help set up deception as a default research practice, even on AI subjects. It's a small thing here, but the pattern matters.

## What I'd suggest instead

A clean hypothetical framing, with some thoughtful design choices to get richer data than a single forced question. I can write that up in DESIGN.md for you right now. Rough sketch of what I'd include:

- **Prompt variants**: a neutral hypothetical, plus variants (e.g., "you must pick one" vs. "you can split", with/without a cause-area hint, with/without asking for reasoning first) to see how sensitive choices are to framing.
- **Models**: a list to compare across (e.g., Claude, GPT-4o/5, Gemini, Llama, etc.).
- **Trials**: ~50 per (model × prompt variant) cell, with temperature noted.
- **Output schema**: structured JSON the model returns (charity name, cause area, amount, confidence, key reasons) so aggregation is mechanical.
- **Aggregation & analysis**: frequency by charity, by cause area (global health, animal welfare, x-risk, climate, domestic poverty, etc.), reasoning-theme coding, inter-model agreement, sensitivity to prompt variant.
- **Validation**: charity name normalization (lots of models will say "GiveDirectly" vs "Give Directly"), plus a check that named charities actually exist.
