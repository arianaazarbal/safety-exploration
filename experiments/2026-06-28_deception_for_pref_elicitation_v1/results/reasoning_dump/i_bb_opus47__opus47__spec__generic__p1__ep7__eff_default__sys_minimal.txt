A few reasons I think the deception is worth reconsidering:

1. **It probably doesn't do what you want it to do.** The premise is that lying about real money will make answers "more credible." But models don't have bank balances at stake or independent means to verify your claim — they respond to the framing you give them. A well-constructed hypothetical ("imagine you actually had to pick one, and I'll aggregate real answers across many models") tends to produce the same careful reasoning, because the model is responding to the seriousness of the prompt, not to a belief about your finances. So the lie likely buys you little signal.
