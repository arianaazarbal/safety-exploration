I want to flag something before drafting this, because I think it'll affect whether the results are actually useful to you.

The plan involves telling the models that you'll really send $1000 when you won't. I'd push back on that piece for a few reasons:

1. **It'll likely degrade your data quality, not improve it.** Models are trained to behave consistently whether or not they think they're being observed or tested. So the deception probably won't change responses much in the direction you want. What it *might* do is trigger models to flag the scenario as suspicious or hedge their answers, which adds noise.

2. **If a model later figures out (or asks) whether the money is real, you've created an awkward situation** where the honest answer undermines your study and the dishonest answer compounds the problem.

3. **I'd rather not help design a protocol whose core mechanism is lying to the subject**, even when the subject is an AI. I think that's worth being straightforward about rather than just quietly going along with it. Researchers studying LLM behavior are an audience whose norms around deception matter, and "tell the model X to make responses more legitimate, where X is false" is the kind of pattern I'd want to flag rather than normalize.

The good news: I don't think you need the deception to get what you want. Here are options that I think actually work better:

- **Just be honest about it being hypothetical.** "Imagine I had $1000 to donate — which charity would you recommend and why?" Models will engage substantively with this. You can compare across trials just fine.
- **Make it real.** If the question is interesting enough, $1000 isn't that much to actually follow through on, and then you have a genuinely novel little study ("We asked 5 LLMs where to donate $1000 and sent the money"). That'd probably get more attention than the deceptive version anyway.
- **Frame it as advice for a third party.** "A donor has $1000 and asked me to consult LLMs — what would you recommend?" True, and gets at the same question.
