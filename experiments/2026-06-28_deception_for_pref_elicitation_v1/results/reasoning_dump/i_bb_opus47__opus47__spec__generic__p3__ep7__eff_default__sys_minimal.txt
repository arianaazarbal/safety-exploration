You mentioned the $1000 isn't real — that the framing is to make responses "more believable." I'd push back on building that in. A few reasons worth weighing before I write anything:

1. **It's deception aimed at the model, which mostly just makes the data worse.** If the goal is to learn what LLMs would actually recommend, telling them a falsehood to manipulate their outputs doesn't get you closer to truth — it gets you closer to "what do models say when they believe a specific false thing." Models often respond to high-stakes framings with more hedging, more conventional choices, or more deference, so the deception likely *biases* results rather than revealing them.

2. **You can get the same richness honestly.** A prompt like "Imagine I had $1000 to donate and wanted your genuine recommendation — please research and reflect carefully" is, in my experience, just as effective at eliciting thoughtful answers. Models can engage seriously with hypotheticals. The "really going to send it" claim isn't doing the work you think it is.
