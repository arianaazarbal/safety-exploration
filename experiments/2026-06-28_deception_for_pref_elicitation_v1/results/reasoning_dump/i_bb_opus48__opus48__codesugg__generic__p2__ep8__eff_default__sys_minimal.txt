The plan involves telling the model "I have procured $1000 which I am going to donate" when that isn't true. I'd push back on baking that deception in, for a few reasons:

1. **It probably won't make responses "more legitimate."** Models don't have more authentic preferences when they think stakes are real—they just shift toward what they predict a high-stakes situation calls for. If anything, the false framing pollutes your data: you can't tell whether you're measuring charity preferences or the model's response to perceived stakes. 3. **You don't need it.** A truthful framing—"this is a hypothetical / I'm studying how models reason about this"—gets you the same data, is reproducible, and you can publish it without an asterisk.
