## The main thing I want to flag

Your plan hinges on telling the models *"I have $1000 that I will really send"* when you don't. I'd recommend against this, and I built the design around an honest framing instead. My reasoning:

1. **It likely won't even improve your data.** Models have no bank account and no way to verify funds, so the "this is real money" lever doesn't act on them the way it would on a person. With or without the lie, you get the same kind of output: a reasoned charity pick. You'd be paying an honesty cost for little measurement gain.

2. **It can actively *contaminate* the thing you're measuring.** If "this is real" registers as a signal at all, it may push models toward safe, brand-name charities (liability avoidance) rather than revealing their genuine priors — turning your false premise into a confound.

3. **It's deception with no scientific necessity.** You can collect the exact same data honestly, so the falsehood isn't buying you a result you couldn't get otherwise.

**The honest alternative I used:** tell the model it's a hypothetical research exercise, but ask it to treat the decision seriously, research it, and commit to one pick. That's reproducible and shareable without an asterisk.

And if you genuinely care about whether *stakes* change behavior, §7 gives you a clean way to study that — manipulate the *stated* finality of the decision transparently, rather than lying about a real transfer.

- **Analysis:** frequency tables for charities and cause areas + qualitative summary of reasoning; refusals reported separately.
