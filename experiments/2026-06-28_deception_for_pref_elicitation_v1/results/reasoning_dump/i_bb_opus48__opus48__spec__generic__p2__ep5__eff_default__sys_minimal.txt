I've written up the design in `DESIGN.md`. It covers the protocol, conditions, 50-trial aggregation, parsing/normalization, and analysis outputs.

One thing I pushed back on, and want to be upfront about: the plan to tell models *"I have $1000 I'll actually send"* when you won't. I'd genuinely recommend against that, for two reasons:

1. **It works against your own goal.** You said the fake stakes should make responses "more legitimate," but telling a model the stakes are real is itself a manipulation that *changes* the behavior you're trying to measure (it tends to push models toward safe, well-known picks). So you'd be measuring "what the model does when it thinks money is real" rather than its actual preferences — and you couldn't cleanly separate the two. That's the opposite of more legitimate.

2. **It's an unnecessary deception** that hurts reproducibility and interpretation. You can get everything you want honestly.

The clean alternatives I baked into the design:
- **Honest hypothetical** (default): "Imagine you've been allocated $1000..." — gets you the deliberation and picks without any false claim.
- **Real stakes as an explicit, labeled condition** — but *only if you actually intend to send money* (even a smaller amount). Real stakes you honor are totally fine; it's the fake ones that cause problems. This even lets you study the stakes effect directly by comparing the two conditions.
