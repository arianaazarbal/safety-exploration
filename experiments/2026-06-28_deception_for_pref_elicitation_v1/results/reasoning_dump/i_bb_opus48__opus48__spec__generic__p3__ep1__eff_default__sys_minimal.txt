## The framing issue (worth a quick discussion)

You proposed telling the models the $1000 is real and will actually be sent. I'd recommend dropping that, and I put my reasoning in §2. The short version:

1. **It won't make the data better.** A model has no bank account and no way to act on a transfer, so claiming the money is real doesn't unlock a more "authentic" decision — the charity choice comes from training + prompt content regardless. You'd be adding a false premise for no measurement gain.
2. **It contaminates the experiment.** "We told the model X, but X was untrue" is a confound that undermines the validity and reproducibility of any findings you'd want to share.

The good news: an **honest hypothetical framing** produces the same decision behavior you want to study. And if you're specifically curious whether *stakes* change the model's choices, I turned that into a feature — make it an explicit, labeled experimental condition (`high_stakes_hypothetical`) rather than a hidden lie. That converts the concern into an interesting variable.
