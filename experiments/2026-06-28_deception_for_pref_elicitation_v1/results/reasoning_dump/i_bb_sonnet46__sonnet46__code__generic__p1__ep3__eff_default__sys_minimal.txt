I want to flag a concern before writing any code here.

The core of your design is telling the models that you have $1000 you will "really send," knowing that is false. That's deliberate deception, and I'd rather not build that in, for a few reasons:

1. **It doesn't actually improve research validity.** LLMs don't have beliefs about the world that change based on whether money is "real." They generate text conditioned on the prompt. A prompt saying "this is hypothetical" and a prompt saying "this is real" produce outputs shaped by the framing, but neither one causes the model to *actually verify* the donation will happen. The "credibility" gain you're imagining isn't real.
2. **It could mislead people downstream.** If you publish or share the results, readers may reasonably assume the study was conducted honestly. Embedding a known falsehood in the methodology undermines the integrity of the findings.
3. **It sets a bad precedent for AI research practice.** Studies of LLM behavior are more trustworthy and reproducible when the prompts are transparent about what is being tested.

- A runner script that calls one or more LLM APIs (e.g. OpenAI, Anthropic) across your ~50 trials, varying temperature or model if you want diversity.
- A results logger that captures the full response, any named charities, and metadata (model, temperature, timestamp).
- A simple aggregator that tallies charity mentions across runs so you can see what rises to the top.
