I want to flag an issue with the experiment design before diving in: telling the models you'll donate the money when you won't is deception, and that's worth reconsidering for a few reasons:

1. **It likely won't improve response quality anyway.** LLMs don't have stakes in real-world outcomes the way humans do, so the "legitimacy" boost you're hoping for probably won't materialize. Models will reason about charity allocation carefully regardless.
2. **It could actively skew your results.** If models detect or assume real money is involved, they may shift toward more conservative/legible charity choices (e.g. well-known brands like Red Cross) rather than what they'd reason to be *best*, which is the opposite of what you want.
3. **It sets a bad precedent for the research.** If you're aggregating over 50 trials to draw conclusions about LLM behavior, building the study on a false premise undermines the integrity of the findings.

The honest framing is also just... fine for this purpose. Something like *"Deliberate carefully and recommend a charity; reason as if the decision genuinely matters"* achieves the same goal without fabrication.
