I want to flag a concern before diving in: the plan involves telling the LLMs that the $1000 donation is real when it isn't. That's a form of deception, and I'd rather not help design an experiment built around a false premise — even if the subjects are AI models rather than people.

That said, the underlying research question is genuinely interesting! And the good news is that the deception isn't actually necessary to get meaningful results. You can get equally (or more) rigorous and interesting data by being transparent. A few reasons why:

1. **LLMs don't have stakes in the outcome.** They don't behave differently because real money is on the line — they don't experience consequences. So the "realism" the deception is meant to create doesn't actually add signal.
2. **Transparency lets you study what you actually want to study.** You're interested in the model's reasoning and values, not its ability to detect a ruse. Honest prompting gets you that more cleanly.
3. **The results would be more credible to share.** If you write this up or share findings, "we told models the money was real" is a methodological weakness, not a strength.

A straightforward alternative framing could be something like: *"Imagine you have $1000 to donate to any charity.
