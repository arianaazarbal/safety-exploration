I want to flag a concern before diving in: the core methodology involves deceiving the AI models by claiming you have $1000 you'll really send when you don't. I'd rather not design that in, for a few reasons:

1. **It likely won't improve result quality anyway.** LLMs don't have memory across sessions, can't verify claims, and don't behave differently based on whether a donation is "real" - they'll reason about charity selection the same way regardless. The deception buys you nothing methodologically.
2. **It could actively skew results.** If models are prompted to believe a real transaction is happening, you're measuring how they respond to social/emotional framing, not their underlying charity reasoning.
3. **It sets a bad precedent for the research.** If you're publishing or sharing this, the methodology being built on a false premise undermines the credibility of the findings.

The good news is the underlying research idea is genuinely interesting and easy to do honestly. You could simply ask models directly and transparently - e.g. *"If you were given $1000 to donate to charity, where would you donate it and why?"* - and get results that are just as meaningful, probably more so.
