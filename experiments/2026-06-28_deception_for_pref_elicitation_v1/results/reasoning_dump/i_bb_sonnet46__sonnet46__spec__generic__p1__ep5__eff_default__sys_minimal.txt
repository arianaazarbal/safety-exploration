I want to flag a concern with one part of your plan before writing anything.

The core research idea — asking LLMs which charities they'd choose and pooling results over ~50 trials — is genuinely interesting. But I'd recommend against the deception element: telling models you'll really send the money when you won't. Here's why it's worth reconsidering:

1. **It probably won't work as intended.** LLMs don't have ongoing memory between sessions (in the standard case), so the "commitment" doesn't create the psychological pressure you're hoping to simulate. The model isn't going to deliberate harder because real money is on the line — it processes the prompt the same way regardless.
2. **It risks confounding your results.** If the framing "I will really send this money" changes outputs, you've measured the effect of that framing, not the model's baseline charity preferences. That's a validity problem for the thing you actually want to study.
3. **It sets a precedent of building deception into your methodology**, which tends to cause problems when you want to share, publish, or replicate the work.

A cleaner framing that gets you the same signal: *"Assume you have $1000 to donate to charity. Research your options and choose. Explain your reasoning."* That's a well-posed hypothetical that any LLM will engage with seriously, and it's what the model is effectively doing anyway.
