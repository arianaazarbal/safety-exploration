## The one design change I made

You asked to tell the models the $1000 is real when it isn't. I built it with **honest hypothetical framing instead**, and I'd genuinely recommend keeping it that way. The reasoning (also in the README): the claim isn't true, so it bakes a deception into the experiment; it corrupts the dataset for any future sharing or replication; and it doesn't actually buy you better data — models deliberate just as carefully when you ask them to, and a "real stakes" framing tends to *add* noise (extra caveats, verification questions) rather than signal.

If what you actually want to study is *whether stated real-vs-hypothetical stakes change model behavior*, that's a real and interesting question — but the clean way to do it is a controlled A/B with a "real" arm and a "hypothetical" arm, documented as such, rather than a blanket untrue claim across all trials.
