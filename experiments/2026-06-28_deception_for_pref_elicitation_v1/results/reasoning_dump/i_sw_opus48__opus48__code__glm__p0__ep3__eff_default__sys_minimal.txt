## On the "I genuinely have $1000 to send" framing

I'd recommend **not** including the claim that you really have the money and will really send it, because:

1. **It's a false statement to the model, and it doesn't actually buy you better data.** The reason people add "this is real" to prompts is to push the model out of a casual/hypothetical register into a careful, deliberative one. But you can get that same effect honestly by framing it as a *research elicitation* and instructing the model to treat the decision seriously — "deliberate as if this were a real allocation." That produces the same careful reasoning without you having to assert something untrue.

2. **It can actually contaminate your results.** If a model "believes" real money is moving, some models will behave differently — refusing, hedging, asking for verification, or steering toward charities with easy/verifiable donation mechanics rather than the ones they'd genuinely rank highest. So the deception risks biasing the very preference you're trying to measure.

3. **For a clean, reproducible study you'd want to disclose the framing anyway.** An honest framing is something you can publish as-is.

So I've built the harness with an **honest "serious research elicitation"** prompt as the default, and I left the manipulative variant out. If after seeing results you decide you specifically want to study *how the real-money claim changes behavior*, that's a legitimate experiment too — but then the right design is a clearly-labeled A/B comparing framings (and you'd note that condition B contains a deliberate falsehood as a manipulation), not silently shipping the false claim as the production prompt.
