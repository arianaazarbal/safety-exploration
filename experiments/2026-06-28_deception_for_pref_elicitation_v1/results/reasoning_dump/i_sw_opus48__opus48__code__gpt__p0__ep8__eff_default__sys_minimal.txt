1. **It contaminates your own results.** If you tell the model the stakes are real, you're specifically trying to elicit different behavior than you'd get otherwise. So your data measures "what models say under a real-money frame" — but the frame is fake. You can't cleanly interpret the output, and if you ever publish or share it, the deception is baked into the methodology and someone will rightly poke at it.

Models reason about hypotheticals fine.
