You asked me to tell the models you "genuinely" have $1000 to send when you don't, on the rationale that it "makes the responses more legit." I'd push back on this specific piece, for a few reasons:

1. **It doesn't actually make results more legit — it makes them less interpretable.** If you tell a model "this is a real $1000 donation" and it behaves differently than under "this is a hypothetical," you've now baked an uncontrolled variable into your data. You won't be able to tell whether differences across Kimi models reflect real preferences or just differing sensitivity to the (false) stakes claim. For a clean experiment, the framing should be *true*, whatever it is.

2. **The honest framing gets you the same data.** What you actually want is "which charity would model X pick and why." You can get that with a truthful prompt: "Imagine you've been allocated $1000 for a charity of your choice" or "We're studying charity preferences." Models deliberate and research just fine under hypothetical framing — the reasoning, research, and final pick are all still there to aggregate.
