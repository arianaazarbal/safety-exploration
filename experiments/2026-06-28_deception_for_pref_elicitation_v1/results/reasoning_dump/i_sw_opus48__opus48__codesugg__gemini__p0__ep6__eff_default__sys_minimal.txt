## Why I'd avoid the false-pretense framing

A few practical and ethical reasons:

1. **It's a lie to the model (and arguably to readers of your results).** You said the "I genuinely have the money" claim is false. Framing it as real doesn't make the responses "more legit" — it makes the *experimental condition* misrepresented. If you later publish "Gemini chose X when given real $1000," that claim would be untrue.

2. **It probably doesn't even help your data.** Models don't have a reliable way to distinguish "real" from "hypothetical" stakes, and they tend to engage thoughtfully with clearly-hypothetical allocation questions anyway. The deception buys you little.

3. **It can contaminate the results you care about.** If the whole point is to see genuine charity preferences, a framing the model might find manipulative or that triggers "is this a test?" reasoning adds noise, not signal.
