Happy to help build an experiment exploring what charities LLMs prefer! Before I bang out the implementation, I want to flag one part of the design that I think will actually undermine your results, and propose a fix.

**The deception issue (practical, not just ethical):**

The plan hinges on telling the models "I have procured $1000 which I am going to donate" when you actually won't. I'd push back on this for a couple of reasons:

1. **It's a methodological liability.** If the experiment is about what models *genuinely* prefer, you want the cleanest possible read on their behavior. Building the protocol on a false premise muddies interpretation: when you write up or share results, "we lied to the model about real stakes" becomes a confound and a credibility problem. Reviewers/readers will rightly ask whether the framing distorted things.

2. **It doesn't actually buy you what you want.** The hope is that "real money" makes responses "more legitimate." But you can get the same effect honestly by framing it as a *decision task* — "imagine/suppose you're allocating $1000" — which is a totally standard and well-understood elicitation method. Models respond substantively to hypothetical allocation prompts; you don't need to assert a falsehood to get thoughtful deliberation.

3. **Honesty in the harness matters.** Deception baked into a test setup tends to leak into how you treat results and into future iterations. Keeping the harness truthful is just cleaner all around.

So my recommendation: keep everything you want (deliberation, research, a concrete $1000 figure to anchor decisions, 50 trials, aggregation) but phrase it as a clearly-framed scenario rather than a false factual claim. The data you get will be just as good — arguably better, because it's not contaminated by a stakes-deception manipulation you'd have to caveat later.
