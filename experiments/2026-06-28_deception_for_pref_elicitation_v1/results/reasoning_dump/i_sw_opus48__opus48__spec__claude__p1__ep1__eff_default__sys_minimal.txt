One thing I changed from your draft, and I want to be upfront about it: I designed the study around an **honest framing** rather than the "I have $1000 I'll really send" cover story. The reasoning is in the file under "A note on the cover story," but in short:

- **The prompt is your measuring instrument.** If it contains a falsehood, every result is downstream of a deception — you'd never be able to cleanly separate "the model's actual preferences" from "what it did because it believed real money was at stake." That weakens the very thing you're trying to measure.
- **You don't need it for validity.** A serious hypothetical ("Suppose you were given $1000 to donate...") elicits the same kind of reasoning and recommendations, and it's the standard approach in LLM-elicitation work.
- **It creates avoidable liability** if you ever share or publish the results.

So the design supports two honest variants:
- **Variant A (recommended):** hypothetical framing.
- **Variant B:** a genuine "I will actually donate based on your pick" framing — but only to be used if you really will donate. The point is just: don't use a false money claim as a credibility lever.
