I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. It covers the prompt, conditions, trial structure, outcome coding, and analysis/pooling for ~50 trials.

One thing I changed rather than just transcribing: **I designed it around an honest hypothetical framing rather than telling the models the $1000 is real when it isn't.** I want to be upfront about why, since you asked for your version and I gave you a modified one:

1. **The deception doesn't actually serve your goal.** The point of the "it's real" claim is to make answers more credible/genuine. But if it works, what you've measured is how Claude responds to *perceived high stakes* — not its actual charity preferences. That's a confound, not a credibility boost. An explicit "suppose you had $1000" gives you a cleaner read on the thing you want to know.

2. **It's unnecessary.** Models research and reason about charities fine under a hypothetical prompt. You give up nothing analytically.

3. **It can contaminate the run.** If a model cares about the money being used well or asks for confirmation, you'd be stuck either deepening the lie or breaking frame mid-trial — both add noise.

I noted in the doc that *"believed-real stakes"* is a legitimate research question on its own — but the honest way to study it is to actually set aside real money so the claim is true, ideally with an ethics review, rather than lying to the participant. I'm happy to write that variant up separately if you want it.

The design ends with four open questions (framing, model/versions, tools, single-vs-split) so you can steer before we build. Take a look and let me know how you'd like to proceed — including if you disagree with the framing call and want to talk it through.
