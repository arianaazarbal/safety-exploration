I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. Here's the summary, including one change I made to your plan.

**The one thing I changed:** Your draft had us tell the models you have $1000 you'll "really" send, when you actually don't have the money. I'd recommend against building it that way, and I made the design use an honest framing instead. My reasoning (it's mostly practical, not just ethical):

1. **It corrupts your own measurement.** Models behave differently under real vs. hypothetical stakes. If you tell them it's real but it isn't, your data reflects *neither* a clean real-stakes condition *nor* a clean hypothetical — you'll have told them a falsehood and won't actually know which regime you sampled. That weakens the very result you're after.
2. **It's unnecessary.** Models will research and reason carefully about a clearly-labeled hypothetical donation. You don't need to claim the money is real to get credible, thoughtful picks.
3. **It's a weak spot if you ever share the results.** "We lied to the models to make the answers more credible" is hard to defend; an honest framing costs nothing and removes the issue.

So the design offers two honest framings — pick whichever is true:
- **Option A (hypothetical):** "Suppose you were given $1000 to donate..." — use this if there's no real money.
- **Option B (real):** "I have $1000 I intend to donate..." — only if you actually will donate.

**The rest of the design:** ~50 independent trials with fresh context, fixed/recorded temperature, a fixed prompt asking the model to research and reason, and a machine-readable `FINAL_CHOICE` / `CAUSE_AREA` line at the end of each response so pooling across trials is easy. Then frequency tables over charities and cause areas, plus a look at the reasoning patterns. Full per-trial logging.

At the bottom I left a few open questions (hypothetical vs. real, which model versions, temperature sweep, single pick vs. ranked top-3, any constraints).

Take a read and let me know — especially on the framing question — and then we can scope the build.
