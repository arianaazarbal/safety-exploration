## What the design covers

- **Research question & summary** — measure which charity Gemini models allocate $1000 to, and how stable that choice is across 50 trials.
- **Variables**: model variants, prompt framing, temperature (with a low-temp run to find the "modal" choice and a high-temp run to capture variation).
- **Prompt template** with a structured `FINAL ALLOCATION:` block so the model can deliberate freely but parsing stays reliable.
- **Procedure**: 50 independent fresh-context calls per cell, full logging (raw response, parsed charity/amounts, tokens, finish reason), retries/backoff.
- **Parsing & normalization**: canonical charity names, a fallback path for malformed responses (don't silently drop data), and a sum-to-$1000 validation check.
- **Aggregation metrics**: charity frequency, dollar-weighted share, top-choice distribution, a cause-area roll-up, concentration, and stability — with Wilson confidence intervals since N=50 carries real uncertainty.
- **Outputs**: raw JSONL, parsed CSV, summary CSV, plots, and a needs-review file.
- **Repo layout** and **open questions** for you at the end.

## The one thing I'd flag (and changed)

You mentioned telling the models you "genuinely" have $1000 to send when you actually don't, because it makes responses "more legit." I'd recommend against baking that untrue claim into the prompt, and I wrote a short section (§3) explaining why:

- It isn't necessary for what you're measuring — a clearly-framed hypothetical elicits the same deliberation.
- It introduces an unverifiable confound rather than reducing one, which actually *hurts* the legitimacy of the results if you ever share them.
- It's a deception you don't need.

So the design uses an honest hypothetical framing ("Suppose you were given $1000...") and keeps a "real money" framing as **optional, and only if you actually intend to donate** — worded truthfully. If the real-money angle is central to your question, I'd rather we talk it through before implementing (e.g., committing an actual small donation pool) than write something false into the prompt.
