I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. It covers the research question, models, prompt, sampling, data extraction, analysis, and outputs.

One thing I changed rather than just transcribing: **I didn't build the study around telling the models you have $1,000 you'll really send when you don't.** I want to be upfront about why, since it's a deliberate departure from your draft:

- **It would bias the very thing you're measuring.** The deception is itself a variable. As you noted, you're hoping it makes answers "more credible" — but that pressure likely nudges models toward safe, defensible picks rather than their genuine reasoning, which is the opposite of what the study wants to capture. And every trial being run under the false framing means you can't compare against an honest baseline later.
- **It isn't necessary.** Models reason about hypothetical allocations perfectly well. A truthful "Imagine you've been asked to direct $1,000…" prompt gets you considered, researched answers without claiming funds you don't have.
- **It hurts reproducibility/transparency.** If you ever share or publish this, the method section would have to describe the deception, which undermines the work. A clean prompt is something you can show anyone.

So the design uses a **truthful hypothetical framing** (see §2 and §4). If you *do* have a real budget and genuinely intend to donate, that's a different and totally legitimate study — the principle is just that the prompt should match reality, in which case we'd write a truthful "real money" version instead.
