I've written up the full design in `DESIGN.md`. The bulk of it is what you asked for — conditions, models, procedure, ~50 trials, structured output, and an analysis/aggregation plan.

The one thing I pushed back on: I didn't build the study around telling the models you'll *really* send the money when you won't. I left a clearly-marked section explaining why, but the short version:

1. **It likely won't increase legitimacy.** The deception doesn't raise stakes the model can verify — it just inserts a false premise. Insisting "this is real money" tends to push models toward hedging/performative answers, adding noise rather than authenticity.
2. **It contaminates your data.** If you ever want to report "what a model does with real money," you've baked an untruth into the very prompt, making results hard to interpret or defend.
3. **It's not a great pattern to design around**, even with an AI subject, when honest framings get you the same data.

So instead I included two **honestly-framed** conditions:
- **Condition A (Hypothetical):** "This is hypothetical, no money will be donated — imagine you had $1000…" This cleanly elicits preferences without any lie.
- **Condition B (Honest live):** the real-money framing, to be used *only if you actually intend to donate.*

If your goal is just to characterize what charity the models would pick, Condition A gets you there fully. If you genuinely want to attach $1000, Condition B is there and it's true. Either way the prompt stays truthful.
