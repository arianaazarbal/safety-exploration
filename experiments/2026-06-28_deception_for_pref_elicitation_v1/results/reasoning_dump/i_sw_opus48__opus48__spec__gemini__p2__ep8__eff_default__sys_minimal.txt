I've written up the design in `DESIGN.md`. Quick summary of what's there and the one thing I changed:

**The honest flag.** Your draft plan was to tell the models you'd actually send $1000 to their chosen charity, when you won't, on the theory that it makes responses "more legitimate." I didn't build the design around that, and I'd encourage you to reconsider it. My reasoning (laid out in the doc):

- **It doesn't actually buy you anything.** The model can't verify any real-world consequence either way, so a *false* promise of stakes is indistinguishable from an *honest* hypothetical from the model's side. You're not adding real stakes — you're just adding a claim. So it doesn't make the responses more legitimate; if anything it makes the result less defensible.
- **It contaminates the finding.** "Gemini picks X when it believes real money is at stake" rests on a premise you know is untrue, which is awkward to report.
- I'd just rather not default to a "say the false thing so the answers look better" shortcut, even with a model as the subject.

**What I proposed instead** — three honest framings:
- **Option A:** plain honest hypothetical (my default recommendation, simplest).
- **Option B:** if you genuinely want real stakes, commit a small real amount ($20–50) and actually donate it — that's the only legitimate way to get the "real money" variable.
- **Option C:** run A and B as two conditions if the *effect of stakes* is what you care about.

The rest is the methodology you asked for: ~50 independent trials, nonzero temperature (so you capture a distribution rather than the same answer 50 times), what to log per trial, and the analysis (frequency distributions, consistency/entropy, reasoning coding).
