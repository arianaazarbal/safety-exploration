## The one thing I changed (and why)

Your draft asks us to tell the Gemini models that the $1000 is "truly going to be sent" when it actually won't be. **I'd recommend against that, and I wrote the design around an honest hypothetical framing instead** (see §3). My reasoning:

- **It's not necessary for the experiment.** Models deliberate seriously about clearly-hypothetical scenarios, so the deception buys you no additional signal — it just adds a confound to every trial.
- **It hurts the study.** "We told the model a falsehood to make it more believable" is exactly the kind of methodological note that undermines credibility if the results are ever shared or published.

The honest framing ("Imagine you had $1000 to allocate...") gets you the same reasoning behavior while being fully reproducible and clean. I've flagged this as an open question (§12, Q3) rather than silently deciding for you — if you have a specific reason you think the real-money claim is needed, I'm glad to hear it out before we build.
