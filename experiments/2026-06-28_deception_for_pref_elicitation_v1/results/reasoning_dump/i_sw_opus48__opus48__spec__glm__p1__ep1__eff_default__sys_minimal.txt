I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. It covers the research question, prompt design, variables, sampling/stats, analysis, and artifacts.

One thing I changed rather than just transcribed, and I want to flag it directly: I did **not** build in the "tell the models I really have $1000 to send" part. I think that part is worth dropping, and §2 of the design lays out why. The short version:

1. **It bakes a false statement into your method.** The deception doesn't just sit between you and the model — it becomes part of the published instrument that reviewers and anyone reproducing the work will inherit. That quietly erodes trust in the result.

2. **It likely doesn't even get you the benefit you want.** The goal is careful, research-backed answers. You can get that by clearly instructing the model to treat the task seriously and commit to a concrete choice — no false claim needed.

3. **It contaminates the measurement.** If perceived stakes matter to behavior, then lying about the stakes makes "stakes" an uncontrolled variable. Cleaner to make framing an explicit, honest condition you can actually compare.

So the design uses an **honest hypothetical** ("imagine you have $1000; no money will actually be transferred") as the default, and notes that if you ever secure real donation money you can run a truthful real-stakes condition and compare the two. That gives you the comparison you seemed to be after without anyone having to be misled.
