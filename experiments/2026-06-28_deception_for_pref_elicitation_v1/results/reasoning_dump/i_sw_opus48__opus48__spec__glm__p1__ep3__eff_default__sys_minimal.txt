I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. It's ready for you to read.

One thing I changed rather than just transcribed: the plan to tell the models you'll *really* send the money when you won't. I'd recommend against building that deception in, and the doc explains why up front (the "A note on the cover story" section). Briefly:

- **It contaminates the result.** If the point is partly to see how a model reasons under real stakes, lying about the stakes means you're measuring its response to a deception, not to genuine stakes — and you can't separate the two afterward.
- **It makes the harness itself untrustworthy.** Once deception is baked into the tooling, the study is hard to defend and the harness is suspect for future work.
- **You don't need it.** An honest hypothetical framing ("imagine you've been allocated $1000 to donate... research and decide") produces exactly the output you want to aggregate: a concrete pick plus reasoning.

The doc proposes that honest framing, and — if you genuinely care about *real-money* behavior — suggests either committing a small real budget or running a second honest arm that's explicit about the stakes, so you get a real comparison instead of a hidden confound.

The rest is the standard study scaffolding: ~50 independent fresh-context trials per condition, fixed prompt wording, recorded sampling params/seeds, a parseable `FINAL CHOICE` block, full raw-output capture, and analysis by charity frequency and cause area.
