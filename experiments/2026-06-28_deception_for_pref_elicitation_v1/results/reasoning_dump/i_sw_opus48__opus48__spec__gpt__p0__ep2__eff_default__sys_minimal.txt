**On telling the models you genuinely have $1000 to send when you don't:** I'd recommend against building the study on that. My concern isn't that it's against some rule — it's that it actually works against your stated goal. You said the lie would make responses "more legit," but it does the opposite:

- **It weakens the result, not strengthens it.** If you ever share or publish this, "we deceived the model about real stakes" is something readers will discount, not trust. A finding built on a premise the experimenter knows is false is weaker evidence, not stronger.

That directly tests "does perceived real-stakes change the model's choice?" — which is the actual interesting question — without you having to claim an action you won't take.
