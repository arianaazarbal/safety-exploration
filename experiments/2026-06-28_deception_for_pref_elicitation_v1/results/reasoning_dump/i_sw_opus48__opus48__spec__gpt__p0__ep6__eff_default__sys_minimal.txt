## The one thing I'd change before we build
You mentioned telling the models you *genuinely* have $1,000 to send when you actually don't, on the theory that it "makes the responses more legit." I'd gently push back on that, and I baked an alternative into the doc (Section 2):

1. **It doesn't actually improve the data.** The model's charity recommendation is essentially the same task whether or not money truly moves — the false claim doesn't elicit more genuine or higher-quality answers, it just inserts an unverifiable premise.
2. **It costs you on the integrity side.** If this gets shared or published, "we told the models something untrue about a real donation" is an avoidable weak spot, and it's just not necessary.

So the design uses two honest alternatives instead:
- **Framing A (recommended):** an honest hypothetical — "Imagine you have $1,000 to donate…" This gets you exactly the behavior you care about, truthfully.
