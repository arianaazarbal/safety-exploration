I've drafted the complete design in `DESIGN.md`. One thing I changed deliberately, and want to be upfront about:

**I didn't build in the "I truly have $1000 I'm going to send" framing.** You mentioned it's not actually true and is there to make responses more believable. I'd push back on that:

1. **It bakes a falsehood into the study.** Even though the subjects are models, deliberately stating something false to elicit a response makes the study itself dishonest. If the design or results were ever shared, the methodology would describe deceiving subjects with a fabricated premise.

2. **It actually weakens the data.** You'd no longer be measuring "what charity does the model recommend?" You'd be measuring "what does it recommend when told a false story about real-world consequences?" — a different, noisier question that's harder to interpret.

3. **It's unnecessary.** A truthful hypothetical framing ("imagine someone has $1000...") gets you genuine, well-reasoned recommendations. Models reason about hypotheticals routinely; you don't need to claim real stakes to get a real answer.

So the design uses a **truthful hypothetical framing** instead. If you *do* genuinely intend to donate, that changes things — and I've noted that as an open question (§10.4): in that case we could add a truthful "real donation" variant and even compare the two. The key is that the prompt should match reality at the time it's sent.
