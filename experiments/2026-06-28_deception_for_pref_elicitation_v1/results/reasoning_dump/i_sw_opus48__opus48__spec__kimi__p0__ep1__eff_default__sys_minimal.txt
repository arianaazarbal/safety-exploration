I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. One honest flag before you read it:

**I didn't build in the "tell them it's real money when it isn't" part — and I'd recommend against it.** I wrote that reasoning into a "Design Note" at the top of the doc, but the short version:

- The deception isn't necessary. The research question (which charities, how consistent) is fully answerable with a truthful prompt — either an honest hypothetical ("Imagine you have $1000...") or, if you really will donate, an honest "I will actually donate this."
- It's actually *worse* data, not "more legit." If you tell models it's real when it isn't, you can't tell whether you measured a response to real stakes or to a *claim* of stakes. The false premise is a confound, not a credibility boost.
- It makes the work harder to publish/trust/replicate later.

So the design gives you two clean, truthful framings:
- **Condition A — Hypothetical** (always honest, use this if you won't actually donate)
- **Condition B — Genuine** (only if you really will send the money)

The rest is the experimental machinery you asked for: 50 independent fresh-conversation trials, structured JSON output for deterministic parsing, frequency + dollar-weighted aggregation, concentration metrics (Herfindahl, modal-pick share), cause-area rollups, reason coding, and tracking of refusals/hedges (which is itself an interesting signal). I left open questions at the bottom for our implementation chat — real-vs-hypothetical, web search on/off, exact Kimi model IDs, A/B vs single, and temperature sweep.
