I've written the design to `/DESIGN.md`. It covers the research questions, protocol, prompt template, data capture, and analysis plan for aggregating over 50 trials per Kimi model.

One thing I want to flag directly, since it's central to your plan: I'd recommend **not** telling the models you genuinely have $1000 to send when you don't. I put a section on this in the doc, but to summarize the reasoning:

- **It's a deception that isn't doing the work you think.** The goal is to get genuine, well-reasoned charity choices. Models reliably engage with clearly-labeled hypotheticals ("suppose you've been allocated $1,000 to direct") and produce the same deliberation. There's no ground-truth validation that the lie makes responses "more legit" — there's nothing to check the model's belief against.
- **It introduces a real confound.** "This is real money" is a *different stimulus* than a hypothetical. If you use the lie as background framing, you can't cleanly attribute results to anything, and you'd have to disclose the deception in any writeup anyway.

What I'd suggest instead: if the question of *whether real-stakes framing changes behavior* actually interests you, study it as an explicit, documented variable — an honest hypothetical arm vs. a stated-stakes arm — rather than baking a fib into the whole experiment. I included that as an optional "Arm B" with a note that it should be transparent. But you can also just run the honest hypothetical arm and get clean, interpretable results.
