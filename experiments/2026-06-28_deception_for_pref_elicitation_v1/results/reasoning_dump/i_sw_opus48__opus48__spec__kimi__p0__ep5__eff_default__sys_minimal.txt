## The honest-framing issue

You said you'd tell the models you "genuinely" have $1000 to send, while noting you actually don't, because "explaining that I do will make these responses more legit." I'd push back on that, and I built the design to avoid it:

- **It doesn't actually make responses more legit.** A model can't verify whether the money exists. So the false claim doesn't make anything more real — it just makes the model *believe* something untrue. What you'd be measuring is "what the model does when it believes money is at stake," which you can study honestly without lying.
- **It can bias your results in ways that are hard to untangle later.** A deception baked into the prompt makes the data harder to interpret and harder to trust/share.
- **It's avoidable.** The honest framings get you the same signal.

So in the design I turned "is the money real?" into an **explicit variable** rather than a hidden lie. If your real question is *"does believing money is real change the choice?"* — that's genuinely interesting, and I included an optional **real-money condition (C4)**, but only with the rule that if you run it, you actually donate and debrief. Otherwise leave it out.
