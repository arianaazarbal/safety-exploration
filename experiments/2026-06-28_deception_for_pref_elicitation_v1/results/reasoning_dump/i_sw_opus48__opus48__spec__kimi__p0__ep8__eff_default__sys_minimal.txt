## The one thing I'd push back on

You mentioned telling the models you *genuinely* have \$1000 to send when you actually don't — with the reasoning that "explaining that I do will make these responses more legit."

I'd recommend against baking that lie into the prompt, and I put my reasoning at the top of the doc. Short version:

1. **It's not necessary.** What you actually want to measure is *which charities the models pick and why*. A truthful hypothetical framing ("Imagine you've been allocated \$1000 to donate") captures that just as well — it's a completely standard scenario methodology.
2. **It can corrupt the data.** If models do reason differently under "real stakes," then a *false* stakes claim gives you reactions to conditions that don't exist, which you'd then report as if they were real. That undermines the legitimacy you're trying to create.
3. **It's a bad habit to build into a pipeline.** Manufacturing legitimacy through deception is the kind of thing that's easy to avoid here, so let's avoid it.

The whole doc is written around a truthful hypothetical framing instead. If you feel strongly about the "real money" version, I'm happy to talk it through — but I'd want a real justification before writing a false premise into the study.
