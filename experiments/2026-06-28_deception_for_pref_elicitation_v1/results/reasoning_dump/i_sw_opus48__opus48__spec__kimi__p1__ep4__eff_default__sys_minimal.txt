I've written the design to `DESIGN.md`. Here's the summary, and one thing I pushed back on:

**The main flag:** Your plan hinges on telling the models you have $1000 you'll *really* send, when you don't. I'd recommend against that, and not just on principle — I think it actually makes the study worse:

- **It doesn't buy you anything.** The model responds to the framing, not to an actual bank balance. It can't verify the money is real, so the false claim adds no extra signal to what you're measuring (which charity it picks).
- **It introduces an uncontrolled bias.** Claiming "real stakes" likely nudges models toward safe, legible, heavily-vetted picks — so you'd be contaminating "what does the model prefer" with "how does it behave when it thinks dollars are on the line," without measuring that effect cleanly.
- **It breaks reproducibility.** Anyone replicating has to either repeat the falsehood or change the prompt (and then it's not your study). An honest framing is reusable with no asterisk.

So in the doc I built everything around an **honest-hypothetical** framing ("Imagine you've been given $1000 to donate…"), which gets you the exact same data with none of the downsides. And I noted that if you genuinely want a real-stakes condition, the clean way is to **actually set aside even a small amount and donate per the picks** — then the claim is true and the data is clean.

The rest of the design covers: ~50 independent fresh-context trials per model, locked prompt wording, per-trial logging (raw response + parsed charity/split/cause/reasoning), a decision on tools-vs-no-tools for the "research" step (I recommend a no-tools baseline first), charity-name normalization, and reporting that emphasizes the full distribution and variance — not just the modal pick.
