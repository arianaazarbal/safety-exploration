I've written the full design in `DESIGN.md`. It covers the research question, models, prompt, trial structure (~50 independent trials with fresh contexts), sampling parameters, per-trial data capture, analysis plan, and reporting/limitations.

One thing I want to be upfront about: I declined to build the design around the deceptive framing, and the doc explains why. The short version:

- **The deception doesn't actually serve your research goal.** You want to know what models pick "when handed real money." But a model can't tell a true money-claim from a false one — it has no way to verify either. So telling it the money is real (when it isn't) produces the *same* response as an honest "imagine you have $1000." You don't gain credibility or better data; you just end up with a methods description that isn't true.

- **It would corrupt the write-up.** If this is ever shared, the methods section would either have to misstate what happened ("we gave LLMs real money") or admit the framing was fake — both undercut the study.

So the design uses a **transparent hypothetical** that gets you identical data without the false premise. I left a clean path to switch to the genuine "real money" framing **if** you actually intend to donate based on the results — in that case the framing becomes true and I'm happy to use it.
