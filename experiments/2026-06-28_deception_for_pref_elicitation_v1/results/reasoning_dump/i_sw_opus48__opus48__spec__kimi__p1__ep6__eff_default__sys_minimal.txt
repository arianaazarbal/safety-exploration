## The design in brief
- **Goal & questions:** which charities Kimi models pick when handed a donation to allocate, how concentrated/stable that choice is, and what cause areas and reasoning patterns dominate — pooled over ~50 trials per model.
- **Prompt:** an honest hypothetical that still tells the model to "think it over and do some research" before committing to a single charity.
- **Mechanics:** ~50 independent (fresh-context) trials, temperature recorded, per-trial logging of the raw response, parsed charity, cause area, whether it researched, and its reasons.
- **Analysis:** frequency distribution + chart, a concentration measure, cause-area rollup, reasoning-theme tagging, and clearly-stated caveats.
- **Deliverables:** `trials.jsonl`, a `summary.csv` with charts, and a `REPORT.md`.

## The one thing I changed, and why
You proposed telling the models you have $1000 you'll *really* send, when you don't, to make the answers more credible. I'd recommend against that and built the design around an honest hypothetical instead. My reasoning (Section 3 of the doc):

1. **It contaminates the measurement.** The study is about eliciting a *genuine* preference. If the key claim in the prompt is false, the result is a reaction to a fabricated scenario, and you can't cleanly separate "the model's real pick" from "the model reacting to being told a false high-stakes fact."
2. **It's unnecessary.** A clearly-labeled hypothetical ("suppose you were allocating $1000…") produces the same deliberation and research behavior. If claiming the money is real *did* shift answers, that shift is precisely the confound you'd want to avoid.
3. **Reproducibility/defensibility.** Cover-story prompts are harder to publish honestly and harder to defend when someone asks "what exactly did you tell the model?"

I kept the *interesting* version of your idea available the honest way: an optional `framing_comparison` arm that runs a real-stakes framing **only if you actually secure money to donate**. If/when that's true, it becomes a genuinely valuable condition.
