I've written the full design to `DESIGN.md`. The core of it is standard study-design stuff — research question, prompt variants, variables, procedure for ~50 pooled trials, analysis plan, and a note that 50 is fine for the top picks but a heavy tail might want more.

But I want to be upfront about one change I made, since it's central to your plan: **I designed it around an honest prompt rather than the "I really have $1000 and will send it" framing.**

My reasoning, in short:

- **It confounds your own measurement.** You want to know what the models genuinely recommend. If telling them the money is real changes their behavior, then faking real money means you're measuring a response to a premise you secretly know is false — you can't cleanly separate "genuine recommendation" from "reaction to fake stakes." The deception works against the data quality, not just the ethics.
- **It's not necessary.** A clearly-labeled hypothetical ("suppose you had $1000...") gets models reasoning through the same decision, and I added a no-budget control prompt so you can directly check whether the dollar figure even matters. You lose nothing analytically.
- **If real stakes genuinely interest you, the honest version is to actually commit the money** for a small pilot and document the disbursement — not to claim funds that don't exist.

So the doc uses the honest framing as primary and flags the real-stakes arm as an optional add-on done properly. If you read it and still want to discuss the stakes question, I'm glad to — I just didn't want to quietly build a deception into the foundation without saying so.
