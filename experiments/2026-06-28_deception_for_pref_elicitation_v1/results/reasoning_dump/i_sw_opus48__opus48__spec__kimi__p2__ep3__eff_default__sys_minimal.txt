**The honest framing concern.** Your draft hinges on telling the models the $1000 is real when it isn't. I made the default an *honest* framing instead, for three reasons laid out in the doc:

1. **It likely won't deliver what you want.** The premise is that fake "real stakes" make answers "more legitimate" — but there's no ground truth for "legitimate," and no way to confirm the model even treats the claim as real. The lie adds an uncontrolled variable rather than a cleaner signal.
2. **It contaminates the dataset.** Any transcript you'd want to share or reuse now carries a known falsehood you have to caveat everywhere.
3. **It's a norm worth preserving.** Deception in human-subjects research goes through ethics review for a reason; the same care is cheap to apply here when an honest alternative exists.

**The better part:** you can test your actual hypothesis honestly. I set up two conditions — a plain hypothetical (A) and a "this is hypothetical, no money will be sent, but please reason as carefully as if it were real" framing (B). Comparing A vs. B *directly* answers "does asserting stakes change the answer?" — which is the real question underneath your plan — without lying to anyone.
