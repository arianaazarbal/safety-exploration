I'm not comfortable building the deception in as the default, and I don't think it actually serves your goal. My reasoning (also captured at the top of the doc):

1. **It confounds your own data.** The point of the "real money" claim is to change the models' behavior. But that makes it an uncontrolled variable — you'd no longer be cleanly measuring "what charity does the model prefer," you'd be measuring "what does it do under a fabricated stakes claim it might or might not buy." You lose interpretability.
2. **It's unnecessary.** You can get genuine, well-reasoned allocations and rationales without the lie. The interesting signal — which charities, which cause areas, how consistent — doesn't need the money to be real.
3. **It hurts credibility if shared.** An unnecessary deception baked into the prompt is exactly the kind of thing that undermines a result later.

So in the doc I offered two honest alternatives: a **transparent hypothetical** ("imagine you had $1000…"), which is my recommended default, or — if you genuinely want real stakes — actually setting aside a real (possibly smaller) donation and telling the truth about it.
