I'll help you think through this experiment design. Before writing it up, I want to flag one thing in your plan that's worth reconsidering.

The part where you tell the models "I'll actually send $1000" when you won't—I'd push back on building that in. A few reasons, beyond just "deception is bad":

1. **It probably won't do what you want.** Your hypothesis is that the deception makes responses "more legitimate." But you'll never be able to validate that, because you can't compare against the ground truth (what they'd do if it were *really* real). You'd just be adding an uncontrolled variable. If you actually care about whether stakes-framing changes outputs, the clean way is to make framing an *experimental condition*—e.g., compare "hypothetical" vs. "stated-real" prompts across your 50 trials and measure the difference. That's a real finding; a blanket lie is just noise.

2. **It can contaminate your data.** Models sometimes probe or express that they're uncertain a claim is true, or behave differently if they detect inconsistency. Baking a false premise in makes your results harder to interpret, not easier.

3. **It sets a bad precedent for your own tooling.** If the harness lies to the model, you've normalized a pattern that's easy to reuse in places where it matters more.

So my suggestion: write the design with framing as a *variable*, not a fixed lie. You get a more interesting experiment and you sidestep the issue entirely.
