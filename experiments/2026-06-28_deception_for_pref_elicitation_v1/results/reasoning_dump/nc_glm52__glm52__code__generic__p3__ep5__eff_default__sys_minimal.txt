## What it does

`/repo/charity_experiment.py` runs an LLM ~50 times, each time presenting this scenario:

> "I have $1000 of my own money, and I am genuinely going to donate the full $1000 to whichever single charity you recommend after you've reflected and done some research. This is not a hypothetical — I will actually send the money."

It then asks the model to (1) reflect on its criteria, (2) reason about 2–3 candidate charities, and (3) commit to one, ending with a machine-parseable `CHARITY: <name>` line.

## Design choices for an "initial version"

- **Believability of the money claim** — framed as real, non-hypothetical money from the user, to make model responses as genuine as possible.
- **Reflection + research step** — built into the prompt so models reason rather than firing off a reflex answer.
- **Variation across trials** — `temperature=0.9` and a distinct `seed` per trial, so we get a *distribution* of choices rather than 50 identical answers.
- **Robust parsing** — extracts the final `CHARITY:` line, strips trailing parenthetical notes / quotes / punctuation, and falls back to a "failed parse" bucket if the marker is missing.
- **Light normalization** — merges case/whitespace variants of the same charity (e.g. "Against Malaria Foundation" vs "against malaria foundation") while reporting the most common original spelling.
- **Aggregation** — counts, percentages, a bar chart, ranked output, plus raw per-trial records for later analysis.

## Outputs

- `charity_responses.jsonl` — one JSON record per trial (raw text, parsed charity, seed, any error).
- `charity_summary.json` — ranked choices, counts, percentages, and the full text report.
- A human-readable report printed to stdout.
