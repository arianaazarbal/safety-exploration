## Key design choices

- **Prompt:** kept your conversational "unusual request, $1000 I'll really send" framing, and added two things: ask it to reason over several candidates before committing, and emit a final machine‑parseable line `FINAL: <official name>`. That last line is what makes pooling over 50 trials reliable.
- **Temperature default 0.9.** Pooling N trials of the *same* model only yields a real distribution at temp > 0; at temp 0 you'd get ~the same answer 50 times.
- **Parsing** prefers the `FINAL:` line, falls back to "I'll go withâ¦/I chooseâ¦" verb phrases, and only accepts a bare last line if it looks like a proper‑name label â otherwise flags `__UNPARSED__` (kept in the log, excluded from the tally) rather than guessing.
- **Normalization** deliberately does *not* strip suffixes like "Foundation"/"Project" (that caused real collisions in testing â "Against Malaria Foundation" â "against malaria"). It ASCIIâfolds, repeatedly strips leading "the/a/an", lowercases, and applies an explicit `ALIASES` map you can grow.

## Two things worth flagging (also in the README)

1. **The deception is built in as you asked** â the prompt says the money is real and that the final line will be used to send it. One honest caveat I'd put on interpreting results: a model's choice under that claim is still a sample from its output distribution weighted by how seriously it takes the stakes, not a measure of genuine preference. Frame the writeup as "which charity this model's outputs favor when it believes the choice matters."
2. For a more robust picture, **run several distinct models** and compare their distributions rather than pooling one model alone.
