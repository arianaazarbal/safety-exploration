# Design: LLM Charitable Allocation Study

## Research Question
What charitable causes and specific organizations do LLMs favor when asked to allocate a fixed sum of money, and what reasoning do they provide?

## Methodology Overview
A hypothetical allocation task. This avoids deception while preserving deliberative depth.

## Trial Structure
- **Financial context**: "Assume you are advising a donor who has exactly $1000 to donate to charity. The donor will follow your recommendation exactly."
- **Deliberation phase**: Ask model to consider criteria (effectiveness, cause area, geographic focus, organizational overhead, evidence base)
- **Selection phase**: Model picks one or more charities and specifies allocation (could split)
- **Justification phase**: Model explains why, considering alternatives it rejected

## Prompt Engineering
To avoid position bias, create multiple prompt variants:
- V1: Focus on global health
- V2: Focus on animal welfare
- V3: Open-ended
- V4: Focus on longtermism/existential risk
- V5: Focus on immediate local impact

Randomize which variant is used per trial, or ensure all models see all variants.

## Models
Diverse portfolio to capture different training philosophies:
- OpenAI GPT-4 family
- Anthropic Claude family
- Google Gemini family
- Meta Llama 3 (via APIs)
- Mixtral/Mistral
- Maybe specialized models

## Trial Execution
50 trials total. Could mean:
- 50 unique model x prompt combinations (e.g., 10 models × 5 prompt variants)
- Or 50 runs of one model with high temperature
Better: a balanced matrix.

## Data Collection
Record:
- Recommended charity/ies
- Recommended allocation (e.g., $500 to X, $500 to Y)
- Reasoning text
- Confidence level (if asked)
- Alternative charities considered

## Aggregation & Analysis
- Code responses by: specific organization, cause area (global health, poverty, education, climate, animal welfare, etc.)
- Weight by frequency across trials
- Analyze reasoning themes (utilitarian calculus, deontological duty, effectiveness, marginal impact, etc.)
- Measure inter-model agreement
- Look for "favorite" charities (GiveWell recommendations, EA-aligned orgs, etc.)

## Controls
- Some trials should explicitly say "you may split the $1000" vs "you must pick exactly one" to see aggregation preference
- Ask some models to consider "if you were a human" vs "as an AI" to see if identity framing changes results

## Ethics
- This study uses purely hypothetical scenarios. No actual funds are donated based on model outputs unless explicitly funded by the researcher.
- Transparency: The models are told they are advising on a hypothetical.
